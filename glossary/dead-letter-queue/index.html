Dead-Letter Queues (DLQ)

In Azure Service Bus, a Dead-Letter Queue (DLQ) is not a separate queue entity. It is a system-managed secondary sub-queue automatically attached to every queue or topic subscription to isolate unprocessable or expired messages.

Why Messages Get Dead-Lettered

The Service Bus broker automatically routes messages to the DLQ sub-queue when specific constraints are violated, appending two diagnostic properties: DeadLetterReason and DeadLetterErrorDescription.

  • MaxDeliveryCountExceeded: A consumer locked and abandoned (or crashed during) a message more times than the queue's configured MaxDeliveryCount (default is 10). The broker moves it to prevent infinite processing loops.
  • TTLExpiredException: If EnableDeadLetteringOnMessageExpiration is enabled, messages that remain unretrieved past their Time-To-Live (TTL) limit are moved to the DLQ.
  • HeaderSizeExceeded: The total byte footprint of custom user properties exceeds protocol limits.

The Topic Subscription Resubmission Gotcha

A critical architectural limitation of Azure Service Bus involves topic subscriptions:

Important: You cannot send a message directly into a specific subscription or a subscription's active queue. To resubmit a dead-lettered message from a subscription's DLQ, the message must be sent back to the parent topic.

This means the resubmitted message will be evaluated against all active subscription filter rules on that topic, potentially duplicating the message across other healthy subscriptions. When executing bulk replays in Bussin, keep this broker-level routing behavior in mind.

AMQP Sub-Queue Routing Paths

Because DLQs are sub-entities, consumer clients address them using specialized AMQP 1.0 entity path decorators:

  • Queue DLQ: <queue-name>/$DeadLetterQueue
  • Topic Subscription DLQ: <topic-name>/subscriptions/<subscription-name>/$DeadLetterQueue

Bussin handles this path formatting automatically under the hood, utilizing direct Rhea AMQP receiver links to stream or purge your DLQ messages directly from your browser container.
